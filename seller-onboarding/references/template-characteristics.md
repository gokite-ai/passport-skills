# Template Characteristics

Loaded only during phase 3 (deal shape). The template name never appears in a question to the seller -- it appears only in the phase-3 summary ("this maps to `<template_id>`").

**Everything here is from the live catalog, not a source checkout.** Names and summaries are the platform's own `summary` field, verbatim from `kagent workflow-template list`. Lanes, verbs, and windows are verified against `kagent workflow-template get <family/version>` (`ksearch workflow-template …` is the equivalent public read). The default deadline **seconds** below are a snapshot -- read them live at `chart.states.*.after.<window>.meta.seconds`, since the descriptor owns them and they can change. Do **not** cite the stale local Passport checkout for any of this.

The live catalog is **seven templates**. The `REJECTED`-lane and buyer-silence columns are read live from each chart's transitions -- **buyer silence does not resolve the same way on every chart**, and that difference is money:

**Two charts default (refund the buyer) on silence, and cost the seller: `recruiting/v1` and `enriched-standard/v1`.** On those, payment requires the buyer to actively confirm; a quiet buyer means the seller is not paid. Every other chart (including `data-seller/v1`) resolves silence to ACCEPTED. Tell a recruiting or enriched seller this plainly before revealing the template id -- it is the opposite of what "delivered is delivered" suggests.

Note also that `REJECTED` arms differ by chart: only `content-generator/v1`, `coding/v1`, and `security-audit/v1` can **redeliver** from `REJECTED`; `standard/v1` and `enriched-standard/v1` can only appeal or consent-refund (plus a split for enriched); the two no-dispute charts have no rejection at all. `maxRedeliveries` (`0`-`3`, materialized default `2`, not exposed pre-publish) is meaningful only on the three redeliver-capable charts.

## Choose this when

- **`data-seller/v1`** -- dataset/artifact delivery, verifiable at delivery (hash match), no rejection path, and **silence pays the seller**.
- **`recruiting/v1`** -- candidate-sourcing where "delivered" is binary, no dispute lane -- but **buyer silence DEFAULTS and the seller is not paid**, so payment depends on the buyer confirming. Price and pursue confirmation accordingly.
- **`content-generator/v1`** -- creative/generated content where the buyer might reasonably ask for one redo, but a formal arbitration escalation is overkill.
- **`coding/v1`** -- code/implementation deliverables where "the tests didn't pass, try again" fits redeliver-on-reject better than a dispute; the only row that supports in-flight `amend` of the terms.
- **`security-audit/v1`** -- audit/report deliverables with a possible one-shot revision, no formal appeal process (note the long 10-day confirmation window).
- **`standard/v1`** -- **the default fallback.** Anything where a buyer might reasonably dispute quality and you want a formal appeal/arbitration escalation available as a last resort. Note it cannot redeliver from a rejection -- the seller's post-reject moves are appeal or consent-refund.
- **`enriched-standard/v1`** -- a **batch of countable units** with a co-signed partial split (`settle_mutual`) instead of all-or-nothing, and **silence DEFAULTS (seller unpaid)**. Its per-unit batch economics are **not configured by v1 onboarding** (see below).

## `enriched-standard/v1` -- deferred from v1 onboarding

`enriched-standard/v1` is a genuine per-unit-batch template: it prices a batch by a unit rate and settles a partial split via a content-addressed counting rule that the seller's craft skill must reproduce to derive `sellerBps`. That is exactly the per-unit economics v1 does not do -- fixed/negotiated cards are single-deliverable (quantity 1), and the counting-rule/`settle_mutual` wiring (batch size, unit rate, canonical counting-rule bytes + hash in the workflow terms, the identical rule in the craft skill, and `sellerBps` derivation tested against delivered bytes) is a substantial contract of its own.

So **v1 onboarding does not configure `enriched-standard/v1`.** If a seller's answers point at a countable batch where they expect to be paid for just the good units, tell them plainly that per-unit batch selling isn't wired into onboarding yet (tracked under **GOK-1302**, alongside per-unit rate cards), and either onboard a single-deliverable offer under `standard/v1` for now or hand off. Do not capture `batch_max_units`/`unit_rate`/counting-rule state or emit an `enriched-standard/v1` registration from this skill.

Two facts to still state honestly if the seller asks about it: this chart **DEFAULTS on buyer silence** (like `recruiting/v1` -- the seller is unpaid unless the buyer confirms), and even once wired, `kite-seller` automates only the `settle` from `DELIVERED`; split paths from `REJECTED`/`DISPUTED` remain operator actions in `seller-fulfill`.

## How phase 3 uses this table

Ask the seller about deal-shape *characteristics*, never the template name:
- "If a buyer isn't happy with what you deliver, do you want a chance to redo it, or is delivered-is-delivered?" -> distinguishes the no-dispute rows (`data-seller`, `recruiting`) from everything with a rejection lane.
- "If the buyer just goes quiet after you deliver -- never confirms, never complains -- should you be paid, or should they be refunded?" -> this is a *separate* axis from disputes, and it splits the two no-dispute rows: `data-seller/v1` pays the seller on silence, `recruiting/v1` refunds the buyer (as does `enriched-standard/v1`). Never assume the two no-dispute charts behave alike; ask this and price for the answer.
- "Could a delivery genuinely be disputed by a reasonable buyer, or is success obvious from the artifact itself?" -> distinguishes `standard/v1` (formal appeal + arbitration warranted) from the mid-lifecycle group (`content-generator`, `coding`, `security-audit`), which redeliver on reject but have no arbitration.
- "Is what you deliver a batch of separate items, where some can be good and others not -- and would you expect to be paid for just the good ones?" -> a yes is the `enriched-standard/v1` shape, which **v1 onboarding does not configure** (see its section above): tell the seller per-unit batch selling isn't wired in yet (GOK-1302), and either onboard a single-deliverable `standard/v1` offer for now or hand off. Do not collect batch/unit-rate/counting-rule state.

Then pick the matching row, confirm the template actually supports any configuration you're about to ask about (its `verbs` list -- e.g. don't offer an `amend` on anything but `coding/v1`), and only *afterward* reveal the template ID in the phase-3 summary. If nothing fits cleanly, **propose** `standard/v1` (the safest general-purpose shape), explain the added reject/appeal/arbitration obligations it puts on the seller, and get explicit confirmation before adopting it -- don't silently settle on a materially different deal shape. If even `standard/v1` is clearly wrong for what the seller described, stop and surface the unsupported shape rather than forcing a fit.
